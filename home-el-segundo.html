Conditions We Often Treat

Common Reasons El Segundo Patients Reach Out

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Numbness in the thumb, index, and middle fingers, especially at night or after a long stretch at the keyboard. Treatment can include night splinting, nerve gliding, and changes to how you position your wrist through the workday.

Carpal tunnel therapy

Cubital Tunnel Syndrome

Tingling in the ring and small fingers, often worse with the elbow bent — on the phone, leaning on an armrest, or sleeping with arms tucked. We work on protecting the ulnar nerve day and night.

Cubital tunnel therapy

Tennis & Golfer’s Elbow

Despite the names, plenty of cases come from mousing, carrying a laptop bag, or heavy pulling at the gym. Therapy focuses on gradually reloading the tendon so it tolerates your real week.

Elbow therapy

De Quervain’s Tenosynovitis

Sharp pain along the thumb side of the wrist with gripping, lifting, or thumb-heavy phone use. A well-fitted thumb splint and targeted activity changes are usually where we start.

De Quervain’s therapy

Trigger Finger

A finger that catches, clicks, or locks when you make a fist — common with repetitive gripping on tools, bars, or boards. Conservative care often includes a small custom splint.

Trigger finger treatment

Custom Splinting

Low-profile thermoplastic splints molded to your hand in the clinic, designed so you can still type, drive, or work while you wear them.

Custom splinting

The Credential, Briefly

What “CHT” Means for Your Care

CHT stands for Certified Hand Therapist. It’s a specialty certification awarded by the Hand Therapy Certification Commission (HTCC) to occupational and physical therapists who have built up thousands of hours treating the upper extremity and then passed a dedicated exam on it.

In practice, it means the person evaluating your numb fingers or sore elbow treats these problems every day. If you’re weighing hand therapy against general PT, our post on hand therapy vs. physical therapy walks through the difference.
