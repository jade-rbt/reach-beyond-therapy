What We Help With

Conditions We Commonly Treat for Palos Verdes Patients

Hand & Thumb Arthritis

Including arthritis at the base of the thumb (the CMC joint), which often makes pinching, twisting lids, and turning keys painful. Therapy can’t reverse joint changes, but it can ease pain, protect the joint, and make daily tasks easier.

Arthritis therapy

Wrist & Hand Fractures

A fall onto an outstretched hand is one of the most common ways people break a wrist. Once the bone is protected and healing, we work on restoring motion, strength, and confidence. See also our wrist therapy page.

Fracture rehab

Rehab After Hand or Wrist Surgery

Thumb CMC arthroplasty, carpal tunnel or trigger finger release, fracture fixation, tendon repair — each has its own protocol. We follow your surgeon’s plan and progress you as fast as is safe.

Post-op hand rehab

Dupuytren’s Contracture

Cords in the palm that slowly pull the ring and small fingers into a bend. Most of our Dupuytren’s patients come to us after a needle or surgical release, when splinting and therapy help hold the gains.

Dupuytren’s therapy

Tennis & Golfer’s Elbow

Pain on the outside or inside of the elbow from swings, serves, and dinks. We use progressive tendon loading and look at grip and equipment so it doesn’t keep coming back.

Elbow therapy

Trigger Finger

A finger that catches or locks after gripping pruners, a club, or handlebars. Conservative care often starts with a small custom splint — one of the many custom splints we make on site.

Trigger finger treatment

The full list of conditions is on our services page.

Why the Letters Matter

A Therapist Who Specializes in Hands

A Certified Hand Therapist is an occupational or physical therapist who has gone well beyond general training: years of hands-on experience with the hand, wrist, elbow, and shoulder, followed by a specialty exam from the Hand Therapy Certification Commission (HTCC).

That depth matters most when the problem is delicate — a repaired tendon, an arthritic thumb, a wrist healing after a fracture. Want the longer version? Read Hand Therapy vs. Physical Therapy, or our post on how hand therapy can help arthritis.
